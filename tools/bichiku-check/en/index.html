Japan emergency supplies checklist: how much water, food and toilet stock?

Preparing for a disaster does not always mean buying a new kit. Review the canned foods, ready-to-eat staples, drinking water, blankets and other supplies you already own. BichikuCheck estimates shortages for your household and lets you consider power outages, water outages and a lack of cooking heat.

How many days of supplies? Example: two people, seven days

Japanese government guidance recommends at least three days of provisions, preferably one week. At around 3L of drinking/cooking water per person daily, two people need an indicative 42L for seven days. This tool's initial planning values are 42 staple-food portions and 70 portable-toilet uses. These are adjustable planning quantities, not proof of nutritional sufficiency or medical suitability.

Everyday food can complement emergency rations

Compare alpha rice, canned bread and hardtack with rice, cup noodles, oats, canned fish or shelf-stable meals. Food needing heat or potable preparation water may not be usable in a blackout or water outage. Check labels, storage and preparation instructions; staple portions and side dishes are not interchangeable.

Can household items substitute for disaster gear?

Existing bedding may help with warmth, and cardboard or reflective sheets can offer temporary support. But insulation against the floor and keeping the body warm are different goals: makeshift items are not automatically counted as complete replacements. Portable toilets likewise require compatible bags, treatment, setup and safe disposal.

How to check your emergency stockpile

  1. Enter your household members, days and expected utility failures.
  2. Check shortages by goal: food, drinking water, toilets, warmth and more.
  3. Record specific products you own and confirm any preparation requirements.
  4. Save or print a shopping list containing only the missing items you select.

How to use it, assumptions and FAQ

How much should I store?

Choose household size, days and expected power, water or cooking failures. See gaps by purpose: food, water, toilet, sleeping and other needs. Starting planning figures are three staple-meal occasions, around 3L drinking/cooking water and five portable toilet uses per person per day. Adjust for personal needs; these figures do not measure nutritional adequacy.

Can ordinary groceries be emergency food?

Yes. Canned, retort and dry foods can complement dedicated supplies. Keep staple portions separate from protein and vegetables, and verify preparation instructions. Cup noodles and some oats or dry grains need cooking or potable water.

How does water rehydration work?

For each water-dependent product, confirm the preparation method and enter the total drinking-safe water required for ALL registered portions. Unchecked products are not silently credited when tap water is out. Cooking water uses the same household reserve as drinking water.

Can foil sheets and cardboard replace a sleeping mat?

Floor insulation and body warmth are different needs. Foil and cardboard may help temporarily but are not credited as complete substitutes for both a mat and a blanket.
